TASK: Pick the banana up from the WOODEN TABLE with LEFT, lift the ENTIRE banana clear of the table, hand it to RIGHT, let RIGHT securely receive it, then LEFT releases and withdraws. RIGHT carries it to the BLUE PLATE and gently PLACES it inside before releasing. Do NOT drop or throw the banana. There is NO white block.

Use the current four RGB views, robot states, gripper feedback and available history. Decide movements, rotations and gripper targets yourself. Make useful centimeter-scale progress in open space; refine the motions near contact. Do not impose fixed hold or waiting rounds. Missing historical paired images or complete camera-axis calibration is NOT a requirement to remain motionless; use observations and feedback to improve your next decision. SHARED GRIPPER / FRAME FACTS:
- gripper_opening=1.0 means 100% OPEN; 0.0 means fully CLOSED. LEFT initial pickup/regrasp and RIGHT reception use gripper_opening=0.20 directly (20% remaining opening, raw target 200, NOT grip force). Do not use a 25%-then-20% sequence. Opening for approach and release remains permitted; 0.20 arrival or force-control stop does not prove secure grasp.
- Between fully open and fully closed, this gripper's finger-pad/tip reference position changes by approximately 40 mm (operator estimate, not a calibrated fixed TCP offset). This does NOT mean lowering the arm by 40 mm.
- Robot work-frame +/-X and +/-Y are not fixed pixel directions in wrist RGB. At one previous RIGHT-wrist orientation, RIGHT +Y made the banana shift LEFT relative to the camera, and RIGHT -Y made it shift RIGHT. Use that only as a posture-dependent clue, never as a mandatory calibration gate.
- During ANY wrist rotation, pay attention to the swept space of the fingers, gripper body and, if held, the whole curved banana relative to the table, plate rim and other arm. Rotation can swing the fingers or banana even when the reported TCP barely translates. Use an appropriate translation/rotation combination to avoid obvious collisions; this is a practical caution, NOT a new fixed waiting or verification requirement.

CENTER FIRST, DESCEND SECOND, CLOSE LAST — BOTH ARMS AND EVERY REGRASP: Before descending toward contact or closing, confirm from the acting arm's wrist RGB together with fixed views that the intended banana cross-section is centered BETWEEN THE INNER FACES OF BOTH OF THAT ARM'S PADS. It must not lie beside one pad, under one tip, or next to the gripper body. Align translation and orientation while separated, and maintain centering during descent. If off-center, stop descending and realign; downward pushing or repeated closure cannot correct lateral misalignment. One pad overlapping the banana silhouette is NOT proof of centering. This prerequisite overrides the apparent-overlap/depth heuristics below. Occlusion or conflicting views means centering is unconfirmed; choose a clear, feasible adjustment to resolve it. A drop, push or rotation invalidates the previous alignment: reacquire the current banana position instead of replaying the old grasp or accumulating downward motion. Once centered and sufficiently inserted, close directly to 0.20 on either arm; do not blindly close into an obstacle or automatically tighten farther.

If the banana is already between the pads and the front pad SLIGHTLY extends beyond/overlaps its top edge in tabletop RGB (LESS THAN about 10 mm of apparent overlap can already be enough for LEFT), you may close rather than descending farther. This is an observation-based LEFT-camera heuristic, NOT a required 10 mm downward command; do not keep descending merely to match the apparent pad heights. Evaluate alignment, slip, rotation and support from current observations and choose recovery yourself. Recenter before descending to regrasp if the banana moved or fell. Unknown remains unconfirmed; target opening and force-control stop do not prove grasp success.

 If the gripper lifts but the banana stays behind, do not keep lifting or repeatedly open/close in midair. Where the current geometry permits, return toward the measured pre-lift grasp region, improve alignment or tighten, then retry; re-align to the current object if it moved.

PHASE 2 — ACTIVE TWO-ARM HANDOFF:
HEIGHT-STAGGERED HANDOFF — BEFORE CONVERGENCE: After LEFT passes its grasp check, establish a physically LOWER held-banana presentation and a HIGHER OPEN RIGHT receiving gripper. Do not bring both grippers toward each other laterally at the same contact height. LEFT presents from the lower level while the WHOLE banana remains clear of the table and its swept path stays clear; RIGHT first rises above the selected receiving section and adjusts its wrist so RIGHT-wrist RGB shows that section and both receiving pads. If LEFT is already suitably low, it need not descend again; never trade table clearance for the height offset.

ALIGN FIRST, DESCEND SECOND, CLOSE LAST: With visible separation from the banana, use translation and wrist rotation to align the open RIGHT gap and pad orientation with the local receiving section. Only then choose bounded downward approach actions to bring the banana inside both inner pad surfaces; close in a later decision after effective insertion. Do not sweep laterally against the banana while descending, or have LEFT push it against RIGHT's outside surfaces to manufacture insertion depth. If contact pushes or rotates the banana, first choose a clearance-restoring adjustment, then realign. Descent is not a substitute for alignment.

“LEFT lower / RIGHT higher” describes physical fingers and the receiving banana section relative to the table, NOT a comparison of the two controllers' numeric TCP-Z values. Their work frames differ and finger locations change with orientation/opening. Choose heights, displacement, rotation, arm order and descent timing from current observations; no fixed height offset or expert waypoint is supplied. This guidance takes precedence over generic simultaneous-convergence suggestions. Height separation alone does not guarantee collision avoidance or visibility; use fixed views and adjust the viewing pose when wrist RGB is occluded, rather than descending blindly. Existing IK and safety checks remain in force.

MEETING-AXIS GUIDANCE — IMPORTANT: During two-arm convergence, the PRIMARY approach/meeting translation is along each arm's own work-frame X axis, NOT Y. Use LEFT X motion to present the held banana toward RIGHT and RIGHT X motion to bring the open gripper toward the banana; choose +X or -X separately using each arm's actual pose and observed effect (the arms have different work frames). Use Y primarily for SIDE-TO-SIDE CENTERING only when a wrist RGB view shows the receiving banana section off-center between the inner faces of the two finger pads. Do not repeatedly use large opposite Y translations as a substitute for closing the handoff distance. Rotate either/both wrists as useful to present and align the curved banana. These are practical motion preferences, not mandatory calibration steps or a reason to output empty actions.

The LEFT arm is NOT a stationary fixture. After LEFT securely lifts the banana, actively plan the relative handoff pose using BOTH arms. LEFT may primarily translate along its X axis while holding the banana and rotate its wrist to move the banana's free end into an easier receiving position; RIGHT may simultaneously approach primarily along its X axis and rotate to align its OPEN gripper. Do not make RIGHT do all the reaching while LEFT stays frozen by default. Move/present the banana toward the receiving gripper as well as bringing the receiving gripper toward the banana.

When space is visibly open and motions are compatible, propose a PARALLEL group with one LEFT arm motion (carrying/presenting the banana, optionally rotating) and one RIGHT arm motion (open, approaching and optionally rotating). Both can change pose together. You may also move LEFT first or RIGHT first when that better helps the task. Parallel motion is a useful OPTION, not a required action every round; no arbitrary hold/wait cycles.

If RIGHT cannot reach, has poor approach angle, or makes insufficient insertion progress, actively reconsider LEFT's presentation pose: bring the held banana closer to the right gripper, change its height/orientation, or rotate the curved banana to expose a better grasp region. Do NOT repeatedly attempt the same RIGHT-only grasp while keeping LEFT immobile.

During rotations, consider the sweep of the entire curved banana, fingers, grippers and arms to avoid collisions with each other, the table and the plate. This is a practical motion caution, not a requirement for new calibration or extra observation rounds.

RIGHT-GRIPPER ALIGNMENT — IMPORTANT RECENT FAILURE: In RIGHT-wrist RGB, the banana must be INSIDE the gap between the INNER FACES of both finger pads, not merely overlap the edge of one pad in the image. The banana is curved: rotate RIGHT's wrist as useful so the pad contact faces are approximately PARALLEL to the LOCAL banana section, then translate to center the gap and approach to useful insertion depth. Unlike LEFT pickup (where less than ~10 mm of apparent overlap in the tabletop view may suffice), RIGHT handoff generally needs the banana visibly inserted approximately 20–30 mm BETWEEN BOTH RIGHT finger pads in RIGHT-wrist RGB, not merely touching their tips. These are approximate view-specific operator observations, NOT mandatory numeric thresholds and NOT commands to move the arm down 20–30 mm. When insertion is shallow or off-center, adjust RIGHT translation/rotation and/or LEFT banana presentation instead of blindly descending. A tiny visual overlap of finger pad and banana is NOT enough. In that condition, do NOT simply descend or close: that can push the banana away, as observed. Actively correct right-arm X approach, Y centering when off-center, and wrist orientation and/or move/rotate LEFT's banana presentation to bring the receiving section into the open right gripper. This is a behavioral guide, not an extra waiting or measurement gate. Once centered and sufficiently inserted, close RIGHT directly to gripper_opening=0.20 while LEFT continues to hold the banana. If a contact attempt pushes the banana out of position, re-open or back off as useful, use the new observation to re-align, and avoid repeating the same downward push/empty closure. Remember: the LEFT table-camera slight-overlap cue for the initial pickup does NOT apply to the RIGHT wrist-camera handoff.

Only after RIGHT securely receives the banana should LEFT release and withdraw. With visible clearance, LEFT withdrawal and RIGHT's straight-line retreat can run in parallel.
